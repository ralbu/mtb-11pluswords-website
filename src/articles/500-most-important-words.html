The 500 Most Important Words for the 11+ Exam

If your child is preparing for the 11+, vocabulary is one of the highest-impact areas to focus on, and one of the most overlooked.

Many children lose marks not because they lack ability, but because they encounter unfamiliar words in comprehension passages, cloze exercises, and verbal reasoning questions. The good news? Vocabulary is entirely learnable with the right approach.

This guide gives you a structured, free list of the most important words for the 11+ exam, organised by difficulty, along with practical tips for helping your child actually remember them.

Why Vocabulary Matters So Much in the 11+

The 11+ tests vocabulary in three main ways:

  • Verbal reasoning: synonyms, antonyms, and closest-meaning questions rely almost entirely on word knowledge
  • English comprehension: unfamiliar words in a passage slow a child down and cause misunderstanding
  • Cloze exercises: filling in missing words requires not just knowing a word, but knowing how it's used

Both major exam boards, GL Assessment and CEM, place significant emphasis on vocabulary. A child with a strong word bank has a meaningful advantage across multiple sections of the exam.

How to Use This Word List

Don't try to learn all the words at once. Here's what works:

  • 5–10 words per day, 4–5 days per week
  • Start with the foundation words, then move to intermediate and advanced
  • For each word: learn the meaning, one synonym, and use it in a sentence
  • Review words your child finds difficult more often than easy ones

Short, consistent sessions beat long cramming sessions every time. Even 10 minutes a day adds up to hours of practice over a term.

Tips for Learning These Words

Make It a Daily Habit

Short daily sessions are far more effective than weekly cramming. Even 10 minutes a day over three months adds up to over 15 hours of practice.

Test in Both Directions

Don't just learn word-to-meaning. Practise the other direction too: being given a definition and finding the word. This is exactly what verbal reasoning questions require.

Learn Synonyms Together

The 11+ frequently asks children to identify the word closest in meaning to another. Learning synonyms alongside each word is one of the most targeted ways to prepare.

Use the Words in Real Sentences

Encourage your child to use a new word in conversation or writing each day. Using a word actively helps it move from short-term to long-term memory.

Focus on the Difficult Ones

If your child gets a word right consistently, move on. Spend more time on words they find difficult. This is how your child makes the fastest progress.
